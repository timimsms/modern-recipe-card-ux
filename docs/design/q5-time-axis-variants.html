Does a time axis improve or destroy the convergence grid?

Column width in a Cooking For Engineers chart currently encodes nothing — it follows text length. A 30-second step and a 40-minute bake get the same width. Six ways to fix that, rendered on the same recipe, so the tradeoffs are visible rather than argued about.

Test recipe: espresso brownies — the chart that went viral on Threads. Its shape is the problem in miniature. Roughly seven minutes of hands-on work, then a 35-minute bake that is 83% of wall-clock time and one sixth of the steps. And in the original source, only that bake has an authored duration; every other number here is an estimate. Both facts matter, and most of the six variants break on one or the other.

What happens when the duration is unknown

This is the constraint that decides the question. In the real Cooking For Engineers source, only the bake carries an authored duration — five of the six steps here have none. The finding that matters most

Every variant that puts time into the column geometry — B, C, and D's rail — fails on the same structural fact: a column is shared by every parallel branch that reaches that depth, so it can carry only one width. The convergence grid's horizontal axis is already fully committed to representing dependency depth. There is no room in it for a second variable. Time has to live inside the cell, not in the grid.

That reframes the question. It was never "should width encode duration" — width cannot, without breaking the format. It was "where else can duration go," and the answer is a glyph.

Does this justify collecting duration data?

Only partly — and this is the part that should go back into the schema. E and F don't need accurate minutes; they need ordinal magnitude and one boolean. A cook reading a bar cannot distinguish 1 min from 2 min and doesn't need to. Requiring an authored duration for every step of every recipe is a large, permanent authoring burden for precision the design cannot render.

Proposal: make precise duration optional, and add a cheap required field.

BucketMeaningRenders as
quickunder ~2 min, part of the flowshort bar
minutesa few minutes, hands-onmedium solid bar
long-unattendedyou can leavefull hatched bar + "walk away"

Three ordinal buckets carry close to all of the decision-relevant signal at a fraction of the cost, and long-unattended is exactly what the cook-mode parallelism banner needs to say "while this bakes, you can…". Precise durations stay optional, used for timers rather than for layout.
